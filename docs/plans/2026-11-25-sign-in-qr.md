# QR from the sign-in screen: the legacy's scan page, a picture or a pasted link, and the root's QR panel finished

## At a glance

What it is for: the sign-in campaign's third milestone, built last. A company's person presses "Use a
QR code" under the card's "or", then scans the code the root shows them with the device's camera,
reads a picture of it, or pastes its link, and is signed in; the phone's own camera opening the link
still signs in at once, as today. On a person's page the root's QR panel gains a copy button for the
link and a question before a new code replaces the one shown. QR becomes on by default, as your yes of
23 November said. Who sees it: every company's people, shared tablets and PCs with a camera first.
The cheapest path: the QR sign-in is built and unchanged; the page calls it as the camera link does;
one small library, jsQR, reads the code from the camera's frames and from a picture, in every browser.
The price: about 145 machine minutes in ten steps, 69 of them before the walks; your online install
of one dependency; one landing with the whole check and a review, your one walk run, four looks.

**Your two asks, each a line of this plan:** "components should be viewable from the gallery before
integration with BFF": step 1 draws the button on today's card, the scan page's states and the panel's
on invented data, before any route; point 2 is the look there. "after finished, walk should be updated

## The brief, checked

**The QR sign-in today, and what it refuses.** `POST /api/auth/sign-in-qr` (`bff/src/app.ts#L416-L458`)
answers one 401 for a wrong secret, a code used or past its 15 minutes (`shared/src/signins.ts#L29`), a
company with QR off, a person who left or whose code changed; it removes the code over its tag before
the session, so two devices with one code sign in one (`app.ts#L447-L452`). The link is
`/login?client-code=<code>&qr=<id>.<secret>`, read by `parseQr` (`signins.ts#L35-L46`); the app signs in
by it at boot (`ui/src/ClientApp.tsx#L158-L198`) and draws the refusal as `QrLanding`. **Unchanged:**
the scan page reads the same `qr` part and makes the same call; a code of another company is that
company's id, unknown here, so the same 401.

**What reads a QR code in the browser.** The platform's own `BarcodeDetector` is not enough: MDN's
compatibility data says of desktop Chrome "Supported on ChromeOS and macOS only.", Android's Chrome from 83, Safari 17
only behind the "Shape Detection API" preference, iOS mirroring it, and Firefox none
(`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/main/api/BarcodeDetector.json`, read
25 November). So an iPhone in Safari and a Windows PC need a library. The legacy used two,
`@yudiel/react-qr-scanner` for the camera and `jsqr` for a picture, the second fetched only when used
(the legacy app's `workspace/uis/base/src/components/auth/ui/QrScannerModal.tsx#L16`, `#L106-L109`).
**Decided: jsQR alone, 1.4.0** (Apache-2.0, no dependencies, its own types, npm's `jsqr/latest`): the
camera's frames, drawn on a canvas a few times a second, and a picture go through one function, the
same in every browser. **A new dependency, so your online install (point 1).** It is fetched only by
the scan page, as the build already splits off any heavy page (`ui/vite.config.ts#L15-L17`), so the
door's own chunk does not grow. The camera needs a secure address, which ridge has and `localhost` is.

**The door knows whether QR is on.** It does not today: the card's form is the same for every company
and only the passkey button follows its options (`ui/src/pages/SignIn.tsx#L138-L152`); the switch is
in the staff setup, read only behind a sign-in. So `GET /api/auth/methods` answers `{ qr }` from
`staffConfig` to anyone at the company's address, asked as the door opens with the passkey's options
(`ClientApp.tsx#L200-L203`); "Use a QR code" sits under the same "or", and the divider shows where
either button does.

**On by default.** `staffConfig` reads a setup that says nothing as `qr: false`
(`shared/src/staff.ts#L163`); the campaign's row says QR comes on with this milestone
(`docs/decisions.md#L18`), a saved choice kept. Every setup saved since the switch arrived sends all
seven switches (`ui/src/parts/StaffFieldsEditor.tsx#L41-L45`), so a company whose root saved it keeps
off; the data set's setup says nothing (`shared/src/demo.ts#L217-L223`), so every walk company turns
QR on, its doors gain the button and the root's person pages the QR panel. Those pictures move.

**The root's QR panel today** (`ui/src/parts/SignInPanels.tsx#L108-L144`): the code, its link in a
