# Every change goes to the activity log, plan, 2026-11-10

## At a glance

**The decision most worth your veto: a person's change is never refused because its log entry could
not be written.** The back end tries the entry before it answers, gives it 600 ms, and on any failure
saves the change anyway and writes one line in its own request log naming the entry it lost. The
other way, refusing a change that cannot be logged, makes the log complete and the app as fragile as
its least-used service; whether a client's data needs that is a business rule, so it is yours.

What it is for: from the first day a client uses the app, every change is recorded with who made it,
when, to which record and what the record was before and after, so the history screen and its undo
can later be built on entries that already exist. No screen shows the entries in this piece.

The cheapest safe path is what is already here: the audit service's own write and read paths,
unchanged, and the storage client's timeout mechanism, copied.

## The slice

A person edits a customer's name in the local copy; one command prints that customer's history,
read through the audit service's own token-gated read path: the time, the person, "customer saved",
and the name before and after. Then every other change the app makes writes its entry the same way.

## What the code says, and what the plan decides

**The audit service already takes the back end's writes unchanged.** `POST /api/activities` on the
keyed listener (`services/audit-backbone/internal/activity/handler.go`'s `logActivities`), behind
one shared key in the `x-internal-access-key` header (`auth.go`'s `KeyHeader`), takes up to 1,000
entries a call (`model.go`'s `MaxBatchSize`), and a 201 means committed. An entry is `clientId`,
`actorId`, `action`, an optional `targetId`, a free-form `memo` and a `timestamp` (`model.go`'s
`Input`). The two reads, by person and by record, sit on the second listener behind a read token
minted on the keyed one for one client at a time (`RegisterTokens`), and every query and index leads
with the client's id (`store.go`'s `feed` and `Schema`). Nothing in the service changes.

**Nothing runs it yet.** The local copy starts c-storage, the back end, the front and the seed door
and nothing else (`scripts/src/stack.ts`'s `startStack`, `scripts/src/local.ts`'s `PARTS`), so the
first step makes it run. On ridge the function's network group sends to c-storage's port alone
(`ops/env.yml`'s `BffGroup`), and no unit, release step or CI job carries the service.

**1. Which changes write an entry, and what it holds.** Every route a person or an operator calls to
change a client's data: the customer and product saves, adds and imports (`bff/src/routes/lists.ts`),
the orders and deliveries (`orders.ts`), the reconciliation close (`reconciliations.ts`), the
employees, their passwords, codes, photos, emails and sign-in codes and the staff setup (`staff.ts`),
the client's settings (`app.ts`'s `ROUTES.config`), and the operator's acts on a client (its
creation, its deletion, its root password). Sign-in and sign-out, the passkey ceremonies, session
renewal, the numbering counters and the operator's own account write nothing: none is a change to a
client's data, and some run on every request. *Reason:* the history screen reads changes to records.

Each entry holds:

| Field | What goes in | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `clientId` | the client's minted id, `request.tenantId` as `resolveTenant` in `bff/src/app.ts` sets it | a code is a label the client may change; never taken from the body |
| `actorId` | `employee.<id>`, `root`, or `operator.<id>` | three kinds that cannot collide; the root has no employee id (`viewOf`) |
| `action` | `<kind>.<verb>`, such as `customer.save` or `order.cancel` | one list, declared once in `shared/` |
| `targetId` | the record's minted id; `setup` or `config` for those two | the history screen's by-record read |
| `memo` | `before` and `after`, the whole record as stored; the person's name as shown then; the outcome | a restore pushes `before` back as it stands |
| `timestamp` | the back end's clock at the change | the service takes a client's stamp over its own receipt time (`ResolveCreatedAt`) |

A create's `before` is null. A memo never holds a password hash or a photo's bytes: the password and
photo routes log the act with neither. An import writes one entry per created record in one call,
since its cap of 500 rows (`shared/src/import.ts`'s `IMPORT_MAX_ROWS`) sits under the service's
1,000. *Reason for a memo rather than new fields:* the service owns no memo schema and returns it as
written, so the history screen needs no migration; the memo's shape has one home, `shared/`.

**Refusals.** A change refused once the person is known (not allowed, a moved version, a bad body)
writes its entry with the outcome and no `after`, as `docs/activity-log.md` decided ("log attempts
as well as successes"). A call with no valid session writes nothing: there is no one to name, and
anyone on the internet can make one.

**2. How it reaches the service, and with what key.** Locally: the stack builds the service beside
c-storage, starts it on two fixed ports beside `LOCAL_PORTS`, on its own database file in the local
copy's data folder, under a key minted per stack and kept with c-storage's keys in the handle file
(`writeHandle`), so a stop and an up keep both. The back end gets two variables, the keyed address
and the key (`bff/src/env.ts`, beside `storageShape`), and never mints a read token in this piece.
*Reason:* it is how the back end already holds c-storage's key. The open listener is reached only by
the stack's own printed read.

On ridge: **not in this piece.** The function reads both variables as optional, together or not at
all, so the ridge release keeps working while it lacks them; until then each change there writes the
same request-log line a failure writes. Carrying the service to ridge is the next plan: a unit
beside `ops/c-storage.service`, one more rule from the back end's group to the keyed port alone, the
key in the function's variables (`ops/app.sh`), and whether CI gains a job, which the move left
yours. Same box or its own is decided by the box's memory reading, still parked, as
`docs/activity-log.md` says.

**3. When the write fails.** The decision at the top. It is what `docs/activity-log.md` already
decided, carried out: the entry goes before the reply, under `AbortSignal.timeout` as
`shared/src/c-storage.ts`'s client does it, with its bound named once beside the new client. A
timeout, a refusal, an answer other than 201 and a missing configuration all end the same way.

**4. Built with its caller, one hook.** A route declares its action and target at its top, fills
`before` and `after` at its write, and one hook in `buildApp` sends the entry before the reply. The
hook is built with its first caller, the customer save; the other routes follow it.

## The human's points

**After the first step: one change, read back.** You bring the local copy up, change one customer's
name in the browser, and run the new command with the company's code and that customer's id (the
last part of the address while the customer's page is open). You should see one line: the time, your
name, "customer saved", the name before and after, and `changed: name`. A yes means a change can be
traced to who made it and what it was, and every other change follows the same way; a no names what
the line is missing, and the first step reopens.

**After every change: one of each kind.** You make one order, edit one employee, change one
employee's password and save the staff setup, then run the same command once for each, with that
record's id: the last part of its page's address for the order and the employee (the password is
the same employee's), and the word `setup` for the staff setup. Each line shows the time, the name
of the person who did it, and what was done. A made record then shows `new`, and a changed one shows
`changed:` and the names of the fields that differ, never their values; a record with a name shows
it before and after. The password line shows only "password changed", never the password. A change
that was refused shows `refused`. A yes closes the piece's reading; a no names the line that is
wrong or missing, and that route reopens.

**No screen moves.** Nothing on any page changes in this piece, so the walks page shows no moved
picture. That green is the proof nothing on screen was touched, not a sign nothing was tested. A
yes is that reading; a moved picture would mean the piece reached a screen it should not.

## What stays out

The history screen and its undo, parked to Friday. Carrying the service to ridge, the next plan. How
long an entry is kept: the service holds 180 days from the act (`model.go`'s `RetentionDays`), and
whether that suits a client's data is yours, not this piece's. One difference from the earlier
costing: a deleted client's entries are not swept with its records, since they live in the audit
service's own file, so they outlast the deletion until their 180 days end.
